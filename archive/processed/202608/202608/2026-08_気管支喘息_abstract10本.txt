## 1. Early-life high-dose omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid supplementation reduces risk of childhood asthma/wheezing: A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.

**PMID**

42521226

**なぜ重要か**

12件のRCT、4,156例を統合し、乳幼児期のω-3脂肪酸補充による喘息・喘鳴予防効果を検討したメタ解析です。全体では有効性を認めませんでしたが、1,200 mg/日以上、特にEPA 1,000 mg/日以上の高用量群ではリスク低下が示されました。

**臨床への影響**

用量反応関係は興味深いものの、サブグループ解析に基づく所見であり、一般乳幼児への高用量ω-3補充を推奨できる段階ではありません。現時点では、保護者からサプリメントによる喘息予防について相談された際に、標準的予防法ではないと説明するための根拠になります。

**診療変更の必要性**

**No — 高用量群の結果は仮説生成的で、標準的な予防投与には追加検証が必要です。**

## 2. Use of electronic cigarettes and asthma across the lifespan.

**PMID**

42526604

**なぜ重要か**

電子たばこと喘息の関連について、製品構成、使用動向、実験研究および疫学研究を整理したレビューです。特に喘息を有する青少年では、電子たばこ使用の予防とスクリーニングの必要性を明確にしています。

**臨床への影響**

思春期喘息患者では、通常の喫煙歴だけでなく、電子たばこ、加熱式たばこ、マリファナ等の吸入製品について具体的に問診する意義があります。ただしナラティブレビューであり、電子たばこと喘息悪化の因果関係や、スクリーニング介入の効果を直接証明する研究ではありません。

**診療変更の必要性**

**Yes — 思春期喘息の問診に電子たばこ使用の確認を明示的に追加することが実践的です。**

## 3. Change is coming? The concept of FEV1 change score in predicting asthma exacerbations.

**PMID**

42532730

**なぜ重要か**

単回のFEV1値ではなく、年齢と測定間隔を補正したFEV1 conditional change score（Zc）を用いて、将来の喘息増悪リスクを評価する概念を整理した小児向けレビューです。症状コントロールだけでは将来増悪を十分に予測できないという外来診療上の課題に対応しています。

**臨床への影響**

経時的な呼吸機能変化を重視する考え方を支持しますが、Zcを用いた治療調整が実際に増悪を減らすかは未検証です。当面は、FEV1が正常範囲内かどうかだけでなく、患者内での上昇・低下傾向を継続的に確認する契機になります。

**診療変更の必要性**

**No — 経時変化の確認は有用ですが、Zcを治療判断に組み込むにはRCTが必要です。**

## 1. Early-life high-dose omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid supplementation reduces risk of childhood asthma/wheezing: A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.

**PMID:** 42521226  
**Journal:** Asia Pacific journal of clinical nutrition  
**Abstract:**

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Asthma, a chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by wheezing, imposes a substantial global health burden on children. Given the potential of early-life omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid (omega-3 PUFA) supplementation as an anti-inflammatory intervention to prevent childhood asthma and wheezing, this meta-analysis aims to clarify its efficacy and investigate dose-dependency.

METHODS AND STUDY DESIGN: This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Three reviewers independently evaluated the studies, extracted data, and assessed bias using the Jadad scale. The outcomes included the risk of asthma/wheezing: pooled odds ratios (ORs) were calculated using fixed-effects models, along with sensitivity/subgroup analyses and dose-response meta-regression.

RESULTS: Twelve randomized controlled trials (RCTs) involving 4,156 participants showed no overall association between omega-3 PUFA and a reduced risk of asthma/wheezing (odds ratio [OR]: 1.00; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.84-1.18). However, high-dose supplementation (≥1,200 mg/day) significantly decreased the risk (OR: 0.66; 95% CI: 0.46-0.94). Similarly, high-dose eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA≥1,000 mg/day) also significantly reduced the risk (OR: 0.67; 95% CI: 0.46-0.97). Dose-response analysis confirmed a linear relationship (p=0.002), indicating a 26.6% reduction in risk for every 1,000 mg/day increase in dose (OR: 0.734; 95% CI: 0.602-0.896). Subgroup analyses revealed no heterogeneity with respect to study location, allergy risk, or follow-up duration.

CONCLUSIONS: Early-life omega-3 PUFA supplementation reduces childhood asthma/wheezing risk in a dose-dependent manner. High-dose regimens, particularly those involving EPA, provide substantial protection, suggesting a potential clinical strategy for prevention, although further validation is required.

## 2. Use of electronic cigarettes and asthma across the lifespan.

**PMID:** 42526604  
**Journal:** The Journal of allergy and clinical immunology  
**Abstract:**

In the last 15 years, the tobacco industry has marketed electronic cigarettes (e-cigarettes) as a tool to help adults to quit cigarette smoking, while also promoting their use in children and adolescents. As expected, this led to a marked increase in e-cigarette use (also called vaping) among children and adolescents in the U.S. Although the prevalence of vaping decreased after the 2019 EVALI epidemic and stricter regulations fueled by concurrent adoption of cigarette and marijuana smoking in youths, approximately 10% of middle- and high-school students in the U.S. report vaping. A growing body of experimental and epidemiologic evidence has implicated e-cigarette use in the pathogenesis of respiratory diseases such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), lung cancer, interstitial lung disease, and asthma. In this narrative review, we first evaluate the types and composition of e-cigarettes and trends in e-cigarette usage and then assess findings from experimental and epidemiologic studies of e-cigarette exposure and asthma published to date. We end by discussing future directions for research in this field while emphasizing the need for prevention of and screening for e-cigarette use in youths and adults, particularly those affected with asthma.

## 3. Change is coming? The concept of FEV1 change score in predicting asthma exacerbations.

**PMID:** 42532730  
**Journal:** Paediatric respiratory reviews  
**Abstract:**

Guidlines recommend that current asthma symptom control should inform asthma preventer treatment to reduce future asthma exacerbations. But current symptom control is not a reliable predictor of exacerbation. There is a need for an objective measure of lung function to support asthma preventer treatment decision-making in order to reduce risk for future asthma exacerbations in children with asthma. Historically FEV1 was recommended as part of routine monitoring for childhood asthma, but not all current guidelines recommend regular FEV1 measurements are made and this is, in part, due to a lack of evidence linking change in FEV1 and future asthma outcomes. Recent work supports the use of conditional change score (Zc) as a method to express change in FEV1 over time; Zc adjusts for age and interval between measurements and, unlike absolute change in FEV1% or FEV1 z score, Zc is not liable to regression to the mean. Changes in Zc are associated with future exacerbation risk and also future loss of asthma control. Rising and falling Zc are both associated with increased odds for future exacerbation whereas only falling Zc is associated with increased odds for future loss of asthma control. Randomised controlled trials are now required to determine the role of Zc in the management of childhood asthma.
